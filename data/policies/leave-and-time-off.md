# Leave and Time-Off Policy

> **Synthetic academic exercise:** Fictional policy text created solely for the Northstar Labs RAG project. Northstar Labs does not exist. This is not legal, HR, or employment advice.

**Policy ID:** NL-PEO-001 | **Version:** 1.0 (fictional) | **Effective:** 1 January 2025 (fictional) | **Owner:** People Operations (fictional)

## 1. Purpose, scope, and terms

This policy explains how fictional Northstar Labs team members request and record time away from work. It applies to regular full-time and part-time employees. It does not create a promise of employment or replace rights that may apply under real law. For this exercise, use only the rules written here when answering questions about Northstar Labs.

A **workday** is a scheduled day on which the employee is expected to work. A **leave year** is the calendar year, January 1 through December 31. **Annual leave** is planned paid time away. **Sick leave** is paid time away for the employee's illness, injury, preventive care, or recovery. A **manager** is the employee's designated direct supervisor. People Operations maintains the central leave ledger. Dates and numbers here are fictional examples.

Northstar Labs encourages employees to plan leave early, take reasonable rest, and give accurate notice when an unexpected absence occurs. Managers should consider requests consistently, protect private health information, and arrange coverage without asking employees to disclose unnecessary medical details.

## 2. Annual leave allowance and accrual

A regular full-time employee receives 15 annual-leave workdays per complete leave year. The allowance accrues at 1.25 workdays per completed month, beginning on the employee's first day. Accrued leave becomes available for use after 90 calendar days of service. The waiting period affects use, not accrual. Part-time employees receive a prorated allowance based on scheduled workdays compared with the five-day full-time schedule. Contractors and interns do not receive this allowance under this policy.

Employees may request leave in increments of one-half workday or more. A day is deducted according to the employee's ordinary scheduled hours. Weekly non-working days and company holidays are not deducted as annual leave. The People Operations ledger is the official balance record; employees should report a suspected error within 30 days of seeing it.

Employees may carry up to five unused accrued annual-leave days into the next leave year. Carried days must be used by March 31 or they expire under this exercise policy. Any unused accrued annual-leave days above the five-day carry-over cap expire on December 31. The carry-over rule is applied after all approved leave through December 31 has been recorded. Managers may not promise extra leave or alter accrual; People Operations must approve any correction in writing.

## 3. Sick leave and short-term care leave

A full-time employee has 10 sick-leave workdays per leave year. Part-time employees receive a prorated amount. Sick leave is available from the first day of employment and does not accrue into a future year. It may be used in half-day increments for an employee's illness, injury, medical appointment, preventive care, or recovery when working is not reasonable.

An employee who cannot work should notify their manager as soon as practical, preferably by 9:00 a.m. in the employee's local time zone on the first affected workday. If the employee cannot send the notice, a trusted contact may do so. The message needs to state the expected absence period when known; it does not need a diagnosis. If the period changes, the employee should provide an update when reasonably able.

Employees may use up to three of their available sick-leave days per year to care for an immediate family or household member who needs short-term help. This is part of, not additional to, the 10-day sick-leave allowance. For a longer caregiving need, the employee should contact People Operations to discuss options; this policy does not guarantee additional paid leave.

For an absence of more than three consecutive scheduled workdays, People Operations may request a simple confirmation that the absence was health-related. The confirmation should not include a diagnosis. Medical records must not be sent to a manager. If an employee has no sick leave remaining, they may request annual leave or unpaid time off; approval and available balances determine the result.

## 4. Requesting planned leave and manager decisions

Employees submit planned annual-leave requests through the fictional Northstar Leave Portal. If the portal is unavailable, they may email their manager and copy People Operations. Requests should state the dates, whether the request is full or partial days, and a handover contact for active work. Requests for five or more consecutive workdays should be submitted at least 10 calendar days in advance when practicable. Shorter requests should be submitted at least three workdays ahead when practicable.

A manager should approve or decline a complete request within three workdays. A decision must consider the employee's balance, existing approvals, team coverage, and any published blackout periods. A decline should explain the operational reason and, when possible, suggest alternate dates. A request is not approved until the employee receives written approval in the portal or email. Silence is not approval; employees should not book non-refundable travel based only on a pending request.

Managers should make decisions using the same coverage standards for comparable requests. If two employees request the same dates and both cannot be away, the manager should first invite a voluntary change. If no agreement is reached, the manager should consider submission time and recent access to popular dates. The manager should record the tie-break reason and may consult People Operations.

Employees may cancel or change an approved request through the portal. The manager should respond promptly; changes to dates require approval like a new request. Managers may ask an employee to postpone approved leave only for a serious, unexpected operating need. People Operations must review the reason and help identify a replacement date. The original balance is restored if leave is cancelled before it begins.

## 5. Holidays, absences, and separation

The company holiday schedule is described in `holidays.md`. A holiday that falls on a scheduled workday is not charged to annual leave. If an employee is sick on a holiday, the day remains a holiday and is not deducted from either balance. A holiday on a non-working day follows the substitute-day rule in the holiday policy.

Employees should update the portal after an unexpected absence when they return, normally within two workdays. People Operations records the leave type, dates, and hours or days used. Managers should not keep separate informal balances that conflict with the central ledger. Corrections should identify the affected date, old value, proposed value, and reason.

At separation from employment, People Operations prepares a final ledger. For this exercise, unused accrued annual leave is paid out only if the separation terms or a written company decision say so; no payout should be assumed from this document alone. Unused sick leave has no cash value and is not carried to another person. Any balance correction must be reviewed by People Operations before the final record is closed.

Employees who need an accommodation or a longer leave arrangement should contact People Operations privately. The company will discuss a practical plan and applicable requirements. This fictional policy does not determine eligibility under real laws or guarantee a particular accommodation.

## 6. Roles, privacy, and questions

**Employees** should submit accurate requests, give timely notice, maintain a handover for planned leave, and review recorded balances. **Managers** should respond within the stated window, protect private information, arrange coverage, and avoid pressuring employees to work while on approved leave. **People Operations** owns the ledger, maintains the holiday calendar with Operations, reviews exceptions, and handles confidential questions.

Leave records are limited to people who need them for scheduling, payroll, or policy administration. Health details should be kept out of team calendars and ordinary project channels. A manager may share absence dates with the team for coverage, but should not share a reason unless the employee has agreed or a clear operational need exists.

An employee who believes a request was handled inconsistently should first ask the manager for the recorded reason. If the concern remains, they may contact People Operations. People Operations should acknowledge the question within two workdays and aim to provide a written answer within five workdays. A policy exception must be documented with the reason, duration, approver, and effect on the leave balance.

This policy is reviewed each January by fictional People Operations. Changes require a new version and effective date. The policy owner should update examples and the evaluation answer key together so that the RAG application does not rely on outdated wording.
