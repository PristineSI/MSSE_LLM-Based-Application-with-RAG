# Remote and Hybrid Work Policy

> **Synthetic academic exercise:** Fictional policy text created solely for the Northstar Labs RAG project. Northstar Labs does not exist. This is not legal, HR, tax, or employment advice.

**Policy ID:** NL-OPS-002 | **Version:** 1.0 (fictional) | **Effective:** 1 January 2025 (fictional) | **Owner:** Operations (fictional)

## 1. Purpose, scope, and operating principles

This policy describes the ordinary work location and approval process for fictional Northstar Labs employees. It supports focused work while preserving time for collaboration, onboarding, and secure handling of company information. It applies to regular employees. Contractors should follow the location terms in their service agreement and the security requirements in `information-security.md`.

A **home worksite** is the employee's approved primary remote location. An **office day** is a day when the employee is scheduled to work from the assigned office. A **fully remote exception** is a written approval to work away from the office on a continuing basis. The employee's manager approves schedules; Operations records standard team anchor days.

The ordinary arrangement is hybrid: teams work in the office on Tuesdays and Thursdays and may work remotely on other weekdays. These are anchor days for collaboration, not a promise that every workday is remote. A team may publish a different recurring schedule if Operations approves it in writing for a specific business reason. Employees should confirm their team schedule before making a recurring personal commitment.

The policy does not promise reimbursement of home-office, travel, or internet costs. Expense eligibility is governed by `expenses.md`. Information-security rules apply at every location, including the home worksite and public spaces.

## 2. Eligibility and ordinary schedule

Regular employees may use the hybrid arrangement after completing 30 calendar days of service. During the first 30 days, a manager may schedule additional office time for training and access setup. Employees should work their agreed weekly hours and remain reachable through approved tools during the team's published core collaboration window, normally 10:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. in the team's primary time zone.

An employee should keep their calendar accurate, mark their location as office, home, or approved alternate site, and attend required meetings in the format selected by the organizer. Core hours are for availability, not a requirement to remain in a video meeting or online without a work reason. Employees may arrange the remaining hours flexibly with their manager, subject to customer commitments and scheduling requirements.

Managers may set in-person attendance for a meeting, workshop, equipment need, or team event with reasonable notice. If an employee cannot attend on an assigned office day because of illness, caregiving, transportation disruption, or another short-term issue, they should notify the manager and request a remote day or leave as appropriate. A short-term adjustment does not automatically change the recurring schedule.

New hires should receive a written schedule, office location, access instructions, and an explanation of how to request adjustments. Managers should review the arrangement after the first month and whenever the employee's responsibilities materially change.

## 3. Home worksite, equipment, and security

The employee is responsible for selecting a home worksite that allows assigned duties to be performed safely, privately, and reliably. They should use a stable internet connection, a work surface, and a background that does not expose confidential information. Employees should not take sensitive calls where household members or the public can hear confidential details.

Northstar Labs supplies a managed laptop and required security software. Employees must not share the device with family members, install unapproved software, disable security controls, or store company records in personal accounts. The rules for passwords, multifactor authentication, encryption, and incident reporting are in `information-security.md` and apply equally at home and in the office.

An employee should report a damaged or missing company device to the Help Desk promptly. Operations will determine whether repair, replacement, or temporary office attendance is needed. Equipment should be returned when requested or when employment ends. Employees may request an ergonomic review through Operations; purchase approval and reimbursement remain subject to `expenses.md`.

Employees should secure paper notes when away from the work area and dispose of them through approved confidential shredding. Printing company material at home should be limited to an approved business need. Documents containing restricted information should not be printed at public print shops or left visible in shared spaces.

## 4. Changing location or requesting a remote exception

An employee requesting a one-time alternate location should tell the manager at least one workday ahead when practicable. The request should identify the date, time zone, and expected availability. Approval is required before working from a public venue, another household, or another city when the change could affect security, schedule, or data access. Managers may deny a location that creates a security or service risk.

A continuing fully remote arrangement requires a written request to the manager and Operations. The request should explain the proposed location, requested start date, time zone, role requirements, and equipment or security considerations. The manager responds within five workdays; Operations reviews security and operational impacts. Approval is effective only when both provide written confirmation and the change is recorded in the employee's work arrangement.

A fully remote exception is reviewed every six months and may be modified if role duties, team needs, law, security, or service coverage changes. Northstar Labs will give at least 10 calendar days' notice for a planned schedule change unless an urgent safety or security issue requires immediate action. Employees may request review of a changed arrangement by contacting Operations.

Employees should not perform Northstar Labs work from another country without written approval from Operations and People Operations. An employee must not assume that a personal trip is an approved remote-work location. This rule accounts for time zones, data access, and employment arrangements; it does not describe real legal consequences.

## 5. Time zones, meetings, and performance

Employees should list their current work time zone in the company directory and update it when an approved location change affects availability. Meeting organizers should consider time zones of required participants and avoid routine meetings outside 9:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m. local time without a business need. When recurring global meetings rotate, the organizer should publish the rotation in advance.

Remote employees are evaluated against role expectations, work quality, communication, and agreed outcomes. Managers should not treat a green status indicator, camera use, or immediate response to every message as a substitute for performance. Employees should acknowledge time-sensitive requests within the team's agreed response window and tell the manager when limited connectivity affects a deadline.

Employees should participate in required onboarding, planning, and incident-response activities. A manager may request office attendance for a specific activity if remote participation would not meet the work need. The manager should provide reasonable notice and work with the employee if there is a documented accessibility or scheduling concern.

If an employee has a prolonged internet or power outage, they should notify the manager through an available channel. The manager may approve a temporary alternate worksite, schedule adjustment, or leave. Employees should not use an unsecured public network for sensitive work merely to stay online; they should follow the security policy and contact the Help Desk.

## 6. Responsibilities, privacy, and review

Employees should follow the approved schedule, protect company information, keep location and availability details current, and ask before changing a recurring arrangement. Managers should set clear expectations, document decisions, and avoid applying different rules to comparable employees without a business reason. Operations maintains team anchor days, office access, and approved location records. The Help Desk supports managed devices and secure access.

Location data is used to plan collaboration, safety, access, and operational coverage. It should not be collected or shared beyond those purposes. Managers should discuss individual arrangements privately rather than posting personal reasons in a public team channel.

Questions should go first to the manager. Requests involving a continuing exception, accessibility need, cross-border location, or security concern should also go to Operations and, where appropriate, People Operations or the Help Desk. The standard decision time is five workdays after the request is complete. A decision should state approving roles, schedule, start date, review date, and conditions.

Operations reviews this policy each January. The approved version in the repository is authoritative for this exercise. Updates must be reflected in the evaluation set and any cached local index before the application is demonstrated.
