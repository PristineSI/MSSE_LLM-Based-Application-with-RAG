# Business Expense and Reimbursement Policy

> **Synthetic academic exercise:** Fictional policy text created solely for the Northstar Labs RAG project. Northstar Labs does not exist. This is not tax, accounting, legal, or financial advice.

**Policy ID:** NL-FIN-003 | **Version:** 1.0 (fictional) | **Effective:** 1 January 2025 (fictional) | **Owner:** Finance Operations (fictional)

## 1. Purpose, scope, and terms

This policy explains which business expenses fictional Northstar Labs may reimburse and how employees submit claims. It applies to regular employees traveling for approved work or buying an approved item for company use. Contractors follow their written service agreement. All amounts are in US dollars for classroom examples; the currency does not imply a real location or company.

A **business expense** is a reasonable cost incurred for an approved company purpose. A **claim** is a request to reimburse an eligible cost. A **receipt** is an itemized record showing seller, date, items or service, and amount paid. A **pre-approval** is written permission from the employee's manager before the expense is incurred. A **cost center** is the internal project or team code Finance uses to record the expense.

Employees should choose a practical, cost-conscious option, use company purchasing methods where available, and avoid splitting a purchase to bypass approval thresholds. Personal costs, fines, and expenses without a clear business purpose are not reimbursable. An approved budget does not remove the requirement to keep receipts and submit the claim on time.

Finance Operations maintains the claim ledger. A purchase is not automatically reimbursable merely because it has already been made. Employees should obtain approval before committing company funds whenever required by this policy.

## 2. Eligible expenses and limits

Reasonable public transportation, standard economy airfare, lodging for an approved overnight trip, and meals during business travel may be claimed. For meals, the maximum is $50 per person per travel day, including tax and tip. Alcohol is not reimbursable. If a meal is paid by a conference, host, or another employee, it cannot also be claimed.

A manager must approve in writing before the employee commits to any single item or related set of items costing more than $250. A purchase of $250 or less still needs a clear business purpose and must fit the team's approved budget. Purchases of laptops, phones, security devices, software subscriptions, or recurring services require written approval from both the manager and the relevant technical owner, regardless of price.

Employees may claim a home-internet allowance of up to $30 per month only if it is listed in their written work arrangement. The allowance is not automatic and cannot be claimed for a month in which no eligible arrangement is active. Home furniture, ordinary clothing, commuting from home to the assigned office, personal entertainment, and late-payment fees are excluded unless Finance issues a specific written exception.

Tips should be customary and reasonable. A tip above 20 percent requires an explanation in the claim. A business meal with a customer or candidate must include attendees and business purpose. Gifts, charitable contributions, and alcohol require Finance review and are not reimbursable without advance written approval.

## 3. Pre-approval and buying process

Before a planned trip, the employee should send the manager the business purpose, destination, dates, estimated costs, and cost center. The manager should approve or decline within three workdays. Air travel and lodging should be booked after approval through the company travel channel when available. If a booking is time-sensitive, contact the manager directly and record the decision in writing.

For a purchase over $250, the request should describe the item, why it is needed, estimated total including taxes and shipping, vendor, and requested delivery date. A manager's approval does not authorize the employee to sign a vendor contract, start a recurring subscription, or provide company data to a vendor. Those actions require applicable Finance and security review.

Employees should use a company card when one is assigned and accepted by the vendor. A company card is for approved business purchases only. It must not be used for personal costs, cash advances, or expenses charged to another employee. If the card is lost or a charge appears suspicious, contact Finance and the card issuer immediately.

An emergency purchase may be made without prior written approval only when delaying it would create an immediate safety, security, or critical service risk and no approver can be reached. The employee should notify the manager and Finance as soon as possible, normally within one workday, and explain the emergency and why no alternative was available. Finance decides whether the cost qualifies under this exception.

## 4. Receipts and claim submission

Employees should submit an expense report within 30 calendar days after the purchase or return from travel, whichever is later. The report should include date, vendor, amount, currency, business purpose, cost center, payment method, and receipt. A trip claim should include the approved itinerary and any required attendee list. Finance may return an incomplete report for correction.

A receipt is required for every individual expense of $25 or more. For an expense below $25, the employee should still retain the receipt when available and enter an accurate description. If a required receipt is unavailable, attach a missing-receipt explanation stating what was purchased, why it was necessary, and why the receipt cannot be obtained. Finance may deny a claim that lacks adequate support.

Employees should report refunds, credits, canceled trips, and duplicate charges in the same report or as a correction. A refund for a previously reimbursed expense must be returned to the company or offset against a future reimbursement as Finance directs. Employees must not alter a receipt or submit the same receipt in more than one claim.

Finance reviews claims for completeness, policy fit, duplicate amounts, and correct cost center. The goal is to approve or return a complete report within five workdays. Approved reimbursements are paid in the next scheduled payment cycle, normally within 10 workdays after approval. These times are service targets in this fictional policy, not a guarantee from a real organization.

## 5. Exceptions, declined claims, and disputes

An employee who expects an exception should request it before spending. The request should name the policy rule, explain the business need, state the amount, identify alternatives considered, and include the manager's approval. Finance Operations must approve exceptions involving reimbursement limits or missing documentation. A manager cannot waive Finance controls alone.

Finance may decline a claim for a personal cost, missing business purpose, late submission, unapproved purchase, missing documentation, altered receipt, duplicate claim, or purchase outside the policy. The reviewer should identify the item and reason in writing and allow the employee to correct an administrative omission where practical. A late claim may be reviewed if the employee explains what prevented timely submission, but approval is not automatic.

If the employee disagrees with a decision, they may ask the Finance Operations lead for a second review within 10 workdays. The request should include claim ID, disputed item, policy section, and supporting evidence. The lead should provide a written decision within five workdays. The original reviewer should not be the only reviewer of the appeal.

Suspected misuse should be referred confidentially to Finance Operations. Employees should not accuse a colleague in a public channel or make changes to another person's claim. Finance will preserve relevant records and follow the fictional company's review process.

## 6. Records, responsibilities, and review

Employees are responsible for truthful reports, timely submission, valid receipts, and obtaining required approval. Managers review business need and budget before approval. Finance Operations maintains reimbursement records, issues claim decisions, and reports recurring policy questions. Technical owners review purchases that create software, device, account, or data risk.

Expense records should be accessible only to the employee, their manager when needed, Finance, and authorized reviewers. Receipts may contain personal information; employees should avoid attaching unrelated card or identity details. Finance should retain the minimum information needed to support the claim and audit trail.

The policy owner reviews this document each January. Changes to limits require a version update and effective date. Evaluation examples must reflect active limits. A chatbot response about an expense should cite the exact policy source and should not infer that an unlisted expense is eligible.
