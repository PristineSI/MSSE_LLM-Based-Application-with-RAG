# Company Holidays and Coverage Policy

> **Synthetic academic exercise:** Fictional policy text created solely for the Northstar Labs RAG project. Northstar Labs does not exist. This is not legal, HR, or employment advice.

**Policy ID:** NL-PEO-005 | **Version:** 1.0 (fictional) | **Effective:** 1 January 2025 (fictional) | **Owner:** People Operations (fictional)

## 1. Purpose and holiday schedule

This policy explains how fictional Northstar Labs publishes company holidays and maintains essential coverage. It applies to regular employees. Contractors follow their service agreement. Dates below are examples for this exercise; they are not an official calendar for any country, employer, or future year.

Northstar Labs observes 10 company holidays each calendar year: New Year's Day (January 1), Community Day (the second Monday in February), Spring Rest Day (the first Friday in April), Midyear Day (the last Monday in May), Founders' Day (June 19), Summer Break Day (the first Monday in July), Labor Appreciation Day (the first Monday in September), Autumn Rest Day (the second Friday in October), Community Service Day (the fourth Thursday in November), and Year-End Day (December 25).

People Operations publishes the calendar for the next year by November 1. If a listed date falls on Saturday, the preceding Friday is observed. If it falls on Sunday, the following Monday is observed. The published calendar identifies actual observed dates and takes precedence over informal calendar entries. A one-time change requires written approval from People Operations and an updated calendar announcement.

A company holiday applies to an employee's scheduled workday. It does not use annual-leave or sick-leave balance. Employees who work a schedule that does not include the observed day should follow their manager's written schedule for that week.

## 2. Holiday pay and time recording

A full-time employee receives the holiday as a paid non-working day when it falls on a regularly scheduled workday. A part-time employee receives holiday pay only when the holiday falls on a day they were scheduled to work, calculated using ordinary scheduled hours. An employee should not add annual leave for a holiday already recorded in the company calendar.

If a holiday falls within approved annual leave, the holiday is excluded from the annual-leave deduction. For example, if an employee is approved away Monday through Friday and Wednesday is an observed holiday, the ledger deducts four annual-leave days, not five. People Operations should correct the ledger if the holiday was mistakenly charged.

If an employee is sick on a company holiday, the day remains coded as a holiday and is not charged to sick leave. If an employee is absent on a scheduled workday immediately before or after a holiday, the absence is recorded according to the actual leave type and ordinary notice rules. The presence of a holiday does not automatically convert an unreported absence into paid holiday time.

The calendar in the HR system is the source of truth for observed dates. Managers should not announce a local substitution without People Operations approval, since inconsistent dates may affect coverage and payroll records. Questions about time entries should be submitted within 30 days after the affected pay period closes.

## 3. Essential coverage and work on a holiday

Most teams are closed on observed holidays. A team may be assigned essential coverage when needed for safety, system reliability, or an approved customer commitment. The manager must identify the covered function, number of people required, contact method, and expected hours before the published schedule is finalized.

Managers should invite volunteers first and distribute holiday coverage fairly across the team. A schedule should be published at least 14 calendar days in advance when practicable. Employees should raise a conflict with the manager as soon as they receive the schedule. Managers should provide a backup contact so that one employee is not solely responsible for an urgent service issue.

An employee asked to work a holiday should record actual hours in the time system. For this synthetic policy, the employee receives the ordinary paid holiday plus a substitute paid day off, to be scheduled within 60 calendar days with manager approval. The substitute day is not deducted from annual leave. No premium pay is created by this exercise policy. Any departure from this rule requires written approval from People Operations.

Managers should limit holiday work to the approved coverage need. Employees should not be expected to monitor routine messages or perform ordinary project work during a holiday because they have a company device. A critical issue should use the published escalation path.

## 4. Planning, requests, and schedule conflicts

Employees who wish to take additional days around a holiday should submit annual-leave requests under `leave-and-time-off.md`. Holiday dates may be popular, so employees should make requests as early as practical. A holiday by itself does not guarantee an extended break, and a pending annual-leave request is not approval.

Managers should publish planned team deadlines and coverage needs before considering holiday-adjacent requests where possible. If multiple employees request the same dates and coverage cannot support all requests, the manager should invite a voluntary adjustment. If the conflict remains, the manager should consider request time and recent access to popular dates, record the rationale, and consult People Operations for a tie-break when appropriate.

An employee who observes a personal or cultural day not listed on the company calendar may request annual leave, a schedule adjustment, or unpaid time through their manager. The request will be handled under the ordinary leave policy. Managers should respond respectfully and consistently and may seek People Operations guidance about a reasonable arrangement.

If an employee changes work location or time zone under `remote-work.md`, the company holiday calendar still applies unless People Operations issues a written local schedule. Employees should not substitute a different regional holiday on their own. Questions about an alternate calendar should include the requested dates and the work arrangement affected.

## 5. Calendar administration and corrections

People Operations owns the official holiday calendar and works with Operations to post it in the shared calendar, HR system, and team planning guide. The calendar should list the holiday name, actual observed date, whether offices are closed, essential coverage note, and version or publication date. The holiday owner should verify weekend substitutions before publishing.

Employees should check the official calendar before submitting annual leave or arranging a handover. If the official calendar conflicts with an approved leave record, employees should notify People Operations with the date and record reference. People Operations should acknowledge a correction request within two workdays and update the calendar or explain the applicable date within five workdays.

Managers should not edit the central holiday calendar. They may add team-specific reminders if they link to the official date and identify any coverage assignment. A local office closure or service schedule should be labeled as supplemental and must not contradict the official holiday date without written approval.

A holiday that is added after the annual calendar is published requires an announcement stating its effective date, affected teams, pay treatment, and time-entry instructions. People Operations should update related leave ledgers and evaluation examples so that the assistant answers from the new version.

## 6. Questions, exceptions, and review

Employees should direct time-entry or holiday-pay questions to People Operations. Coverage questions should go to the manager. People Operations should answer within five workdays and include a calendar reference or applicable policy section. If the question concerns a balance, the response should specify the corrected code and date.

An exception request must identify the holiday, affected work group, operational reason, proposed treatment, and requested duration. People Operations must approve any change to the official observed date, paid status, or substitute-day rule in writing. A manager may approve a coverage schedule but cannot change the company-wide holiday calendar.

People Operations reviews this policy every January and publishes the next year's dates by November 1. Each update should include a version number and effective date. This policy should remain consistent with `leave-and-time-off.md`, especially the rule that holidays are not deducted from annual leave, and with `remote-work.md` for location schedules.

For RAG evaluation, the holiday calendar should be tested with questions about weekend substitution, annual-leave deductions, holiday coverage, and time-zone changes. Answers should distinguish a published company holiday from a personal observance and cite this document or the official calendar record.
