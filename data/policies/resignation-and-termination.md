# Resignation and Termination of Appointment Policy

> **Synthetic academic exercise:** Fictional policy text created solely for the Northstar Labs RAG project. Northstar Labs does not exist. This document is not legal, HR, or employment advice and does not describe real employment rights or obligations.

**Policy ID:** NL-PEO-006 | **Version:** 1.0 (fictional) | **Effective:** 1 January 2025 (fictional) | **Owner:** People Operations (fictional)

## 1. Purpose, scope, and terms

This policy describes internal planning steps when an appointment with fictional Northstar Labs ends. It covers employee-initiated resignation and company-initiated termination of appointment. It is a classroom scenario, not a model contract. Real employers would need qualified review for the applicable location and individual agreement; this document intentionally does not state legal notice rules, grounds for dismissal, final-pay entitlements, or appeal rights.

**Resignation** means an employee's written notice that they intend to end their appointment. **Termination of appointment** means the company's written decision to end the appointment. **Last working day** means the final day on which the person is expected to perform work. **Separation date** means the effective end date recorded by People Operations. These dates may differ if the company places a person on leave or changes duties during a transition.

The goals are to communicate clearly, maintain respectful treatment, protect company and personal information, transfer work responsibly, and close accounts and property records. People Operations coordinates the process and keeps the official separation record. A manager should not announce a separation before People Operations confirms the communication plan.

## 2. Employee resignation process

An employee who decides to resign should send a dated written notice to their manager and People Operations. The notice should identify the intended last working day, a preferred contact method, and any transition matters the employee wants to raise. Employees may ask People Operations privately about how their written appointment documents apply. This policy does not set a universal notice period; the employee's written appointment documents and an authorized People Operations response control the fictional company's planning record.

The manager should acknowledge receipt within two workdays and arrange a private conversation if the employee wishes to discuss transition timing. The manager should not pressure the employee to withdraw a notice or promise a change to the separation date without People Operations approval. People Operations should confirm the recorded separation date and next steps in writing within five workdays after receiving the notice or after the date has been agreed.

During the transition, the employee and manager should identify active projects, deadlines, key contacts, open decisions, and records that need a new owner. The handover should contain business information only and should be stored in an approved company location. Employees should not transfer company files to a personal account or retain copies after access ends. The manager is responsible for assigning a successor or recording that no successor is needed.

An employee may ask to revise or withdraw a resignation before the separation date. The request should be made in writing to People Operations. A change is effective only after People Operations confirms it in writing; a manager's informal statement is not confirmation. People Operations records the decision and informs the manager and relevant operations staff.

## 3. Company-initiated termination process

A company-initiated termination must be authorized by the designated department leader and People Operations before it is communicated. The decision record should identify the approving roles, effective date, communication owner, return-of-property plan, access plan, and any follow-up contact. The policy does not define substantive grounds or a legal process for termination.

When practical and appropriate to the scenario, the manager and People Operations should hold a private meeting with the employee, explain the company's decision in clear terms, provide a written summary, and identify whom the employee may contact with administrative questions. The meeting should not disclose another person's private information. If an immediate security or safety concern exists, Information Security or Operations may recommend changing access or work duties before the meeting; People Operations coordinates the timing and communication.

The company may place an employee on a transition status, reassign duties, or end system access at a scheduled time. These are separate decisions and should be recorded separately. Access changes must follow `information-security.md`. The manager should arrange coverage without directing coworkers to speculate about the reason for the separation.

If an employee asks for clarification of an administrative record, People Operations should acknowledge the question within two workdays and aim to respond within five workdays. This internal question process does not create an appeal right or change a separation decision. The document intentionally leaves legal review, statutory procedures, severance, final wages, benefits continuation, and disputes to appropriate real-world advice and applicable written terms.

## 4. Final work, property, and account closure

Before the separation date, the manager should confirm the status of company work, document owners, recurring meetings, customer or vendor handoffs, and any deadlines that remain. Employees should return company property, including managed devices, access cards, security keys, and other issued items, using the method provided by Operations. The property checklist should record the item, return date, and receiving role. A missing or damaged item should be reported honestly; employees should not erase or repair a company device themselves.

Operations maintains the return checklist. The Help Desk disables or changes accounts at the time specified by People Operations and Information Security. A manager may not keep an account active for convenience after the approved closure time. If a temporary extension is needed for a documented handover, the system owner must approve a limited scope and end date in writing. The access should be reviewed when the extension expires.

People Operations asks Finance to reconcile outstanding expense reports under `expenses.md` and time entries under the applicable leave and holiday policies. This policy does not promise a particular payment, deduction, benefit, or balance treatment. Questions about such records should be routed to the responsible team and answered using the applicable written policy or appointment record.

Company files must remain in company systems. The departing employee should not delete shared business records, take confidential material, or access systems after permission ends. The manager should preserve records needed for continuity and follow the retention instructions issued by the system owner. Personal belongings should be returned through a private, respectful process.

## 5. Communications, privacy, and respectful treatment

People Operations approves the internal communication about a departure. The message should normally contain only the person's name if authorized, last working day or transition date if appropriate, handover contact, and a neutral appreciation or factual statement. It should not disclose confidential details, health information, performance allegations, or another person's private data.

Employees may ask that a personal contact method be used for follow-up about administrative records. People Operations should store that contact information separately from public team announcements and share it only with staff who need it for the separation process. Managers and coworkers should not request personal passwords or use a departing employee's identity to continue work.

All parties should keep conversations professional. A departure should not be used to threaten, shame, or retaliate against a person. Concerns about the process may be sent privately to People Operations. The assigned People Operations representative should record the concern, route it to an appropriate reviewer, and acknowledge receipt within two workdays.

Information Security handles suspected data loss or account misuse under `information-security.md`; the separation process should not replace incident response. If the departing employee reports a security concern, the manager should direct them promptly to the Help Desk and Information Security.

## 6. Responsibilities, records, and review

The employee provides accurate transition information and returns company property. The manager plans work coverage and keeps communication respectful. People Operations owns the separation record, confirms dates and notices, coordinates relevant teams, and responds to administrative questions. Operations tracks issued property. Finance reconciles expense and time records. The Help Desk and system owners manage access according to written instructions from People Operations and Information Security.

The separation record should include the written notice or company decision, confirmed dates, required approvals, communication checklist, handover owner, property status, and account-closure confirmation. Access is restricted to authorized personnel. Sensitive personal details should not be copied into broad team records.

People Operations reviews this policy each January. Updates require a new version and effective date and should be reviewed for consistency with the security, leave, holiday, remote-work, and expense policies. Because this document omits jurisdiction-specific rights and obligations, the RAG application should refuse questions asking for legal advice or a determination of an individual's real-world entitlements.
